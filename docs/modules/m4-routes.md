# Module 4 · Terrain, radar, subsurface evidence, and route exploration

Module 4 turns a published Earth DEM window into an exploratory route and evidence view. It supports all Module 1 discovery candidates and the four fixed Earth reference windows. A route is a regional corridor hypothesis for follow-up study. It is not rover clearance, a field-tested trail, or a planetary landing assessment.

## User-facing capabilities

| View | Available data and calculation | Scope and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Earth terrain grid | Horn 3×3 slope, 3×3 local relief (max minus min), TRI (mean absolute center-to-neighbor difference), elevation and approximate cell coordinates | Discovery and fixed-reference Earth DEMs. Native DEM resolution is usually 30 m; the displayed grid is 37.5 m. Borders and nodata neighborhoods remain unknown. |
| One-meter local terrain | A 401×401 bare-earth USGS OPR DEM crop, native Horn slope, 3×3 local relief, and a local terrain-break residual screen | A 400 m patch centered on `cand-valles-marineris-05` only. It does not change the 2,400 m regional route calculation, identify individual objects, or establish vehicle clearance. |
| Routes | Two cost settings compare distance and slope under a user-set maximum slope. Optional local-relief limit excludes cells, including unknown relief. Reports path, distance, ascent/descent, slope, relief, and TRI. | Limits are scenario settings, not validated vehicle specifications. No rock, soil-bearing, crevasse, or cave-roof detector is included. |
| Terrain shadow | DEM ray tracing at manual sun angles or a UTC-derived Earth sun position | A modeled, window-limited layer. It does not constrain the route or include terrain outside the grid. UTC sun mode applies to Earth only. |
| Sentinel-1 time series | The existing Module 3 12-month window-mean RTC history is validated against the selected reference's ID, center, extent, polarization, orbit, dates, and coverage. | Four fixed Earth references only. Window means are not a pixel map; mixed platforms are not treated as directly comparable pairs. |
| Spatial Sentinel-1 map | Two same-platform, same-polarization, same-orbit RTC scenes per Earth window. Displays the later paired scene's backscatter and a repeat-pass surface difference with georeferencing, masks, coverage, dates, resolution, source scene links, and attribution. | All 19 published Discovery candidates and four fixed Earth references have their own maps. A 3 dB display threshold is an unvalidated surface-change screen, not a hazard or void detector. |
| Marius Hills pit | LROC published point, funnel/opening dimensions and 40 m stereo-modeled pit depth; a separate 4 m/pixel LROC surface DTM crop with elevation, Horn slope, and local relief views. | The target's regional Kaguya/LOLA grid center is about 204 m from the mapped pit. The DTM has nodata at the pit and does not independently measure its depth or an underground lava tube. |
| La Corona | Published cave reference point; 74 digitized metric interior cross-sections from Pyroduct v3, with width/height/area and a selectable outline; cited ERT ceiling-depth range for separate channels | Sections have no georeferenced stations or roof depths. The 14–29 m ERT range applies to inferred secondary channels, not every section. No tube polygon, 3-D interior route, or roof-stability model is attached. |

## Spatial SAR publication

`scripts/m4_build_sar_maps.py` and `scripts/m4_build_discovery_sar.py` use the public [Planetary Computer Sentinel-1 RTC collection](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/dataset/sentinel-1-rtc), its STAC API, and its numeric Data API. Both use only the Python standard library. Run `python3 scripts/m4_build_sar_maps.py [--force] [reference-site ...]` for the four fixed references and `python3 scripts/m4_build_discovery_sar.py --as-of 2026-11-15 [--force] [candidate-id ...]` for the 19 published Discovery candidates. Output is `assets/data/m4/<site-id>-sar.json`. The scripts do not store an access token, COG URL signature, or credential. Each output records the two exact STAC scene URLs, `fetchedAt`, collection, credit, CC-BY-4.0 license, and method.

For fixed references, selection starts from the published Module 3 process history. For Discovery candidates, a separate STAC search covers the candidate's exact center/window and the 90 days preceding `--as-of`; each output stores that interval. No reference SAR scene is copied to a candidate. The newest qualifying pair within 24 days must share platform, VV or HH polarization, relative orbit, pass direction, instrument mode, observation direction, source pixel spacing, and nominal radar resolution. Each scene is checked against STAC metadata. The service returns a 120×120 numeric crop of the 2400 m Earth window at about 20 m output sample spacing. The source RTC assets have 10 m pixel spacing, while the IW radar's nominal spatial resolution in the reviewed scenes is about 20 m in range and 22 m in azimuth; pixel spacing must not be described as resolved detail. A pixel is valid only when its RTC gamma0 power is positive and its returned mask is valid. Both scenes and the paired difference require at least 90% coverage. The later paired scene map uses `10 log10(gamma0)` dB; this scene need not be the latest acquisition in the full history. The difference takes a 5×5 local mean in **linear power** in each date, then `10 log10(new/old)`; null remains unknown. At 20 m output sample spacing, that smoothing footprint is about 100 m.

The map is a surface radar context layer. Rain, surface moisture, roughness, incidence geometry, and processing can affect backscatter. Same-orbit pairing reduces geometric differences but does not supply a per-pixel incidence-angle layer; the selected STAC assets provide no such raster. The displayed fraction exceeding 3 dB is descriptive and has not been validated with independent labels. It must not be read as a cavity, obstacle, sinkhole, or route-safety probability. Terrestrial Sentinel-1 dB and lunar Mini-RF CPR are different measurements and are never numerically compared.

The published file schema is `M4SpatialSarPair` version 1. It includes `siteId`, `siteRole`, optional `targetId` and `searchInterval`, `center`, `bboxWgs84`, `gridSize`, `windowMeters`, source pixel spacing, nominal radar resolution, output sample spacing, pair geometry, per-scene date/ID/coverage/source, `latestDb`, `deltaDb`, `comparableFraction`, display threshold/fraction, provenance, method, and caveats. `m4-sar.mjs` rejects mismatched site, candidate target, center, window, polarization, date, coverage, and malformed arrays before presentation. This is an additive M4 format; it does not alter `CandidateAnalogPayload`.

The explorer lets users download this georeferenced SAR JSON for the selected site. The browser does not fetch COGs at runtime; published static JSON makes the map usable on static hosting.

## Lunar pit geometry and depth provenance

`scripts/m4_extract_lroc_dtm.py` validates the source GeoTIFF layout and Moon equirectangular geokeys, then extracts a 101×101 native 4 m crop around the [LROC Pits Atlas Marius Hills point](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/atlases/pits/1). Its output is `assets/data/m4/marius-pit-dtm.json` (`M4SurfaceDtmCrop` version 1) with source URL, SHA-256, lunar projection, pit point and pixel position, elevation or null, and caveats. It is reproducible from the [NAC_DTM_MARIUSPIT01 product](https://data.lroc.im-ldi.com/lroc/view_rdr/NAC_DTM_MARIUSPIT01) using `python3 scripts/m4_extract_lroc_dtm.py --input <downloaded-TIF>`.

The atlas reports a 40 m **surface pit depth from stereo modeling**, funnel diameters 79×92 m, inner opening 49×55 m, and azimuth 65°. The cited record does not report a depth uncertainty. Module 4 also calculates approximate plan-view areas by treating each diameter pair as perpendicular ellipse axes; these are labeled geometric assumptions, not measured area or volume. The map ring is the published point. Dark DTM pixels are missing surface elevation, not a mapped hole boundary. Derived 4 m surface slope and local relief may reveal terrain morphology around the pit but do not define entrance accessibility, roof thickness, the depth of a tube, or its interior. There is no source-backed underground geometry in this product.

## La Corona interior sections and depth limits

`scripts/m4_extract_pyroduct_corona.py` reads the open [Pyroduct Digital Catalog v3.0](https://zenodo.org/records/23456401) ZIP (CC BY 4.0) and publishes the 74 `ES.LNZ.00` La Corona cross-sections as `assets/data/m4/la-corona-cave-sections.json` (`M4CaveSections` version 1). Download the original archive, then run `python3 scripts/m4_extract_pyroduct_corona.py --input <downloaded-ZIP>`. The output records the archive SHA-256, original catalog code, survey attribution, source links, independent local metric outlines, and each outline's width, height, and shoelace area. The section drawings are digitized from the [Tomasi et al. (2022) La Corona study](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022JB024056), whose underlying survey used terrestrial laser scanning. The original full 3-D interior model remains access restricted; these published sections are a usable but narrower geometry product.

The 74 selected outlines span **3.73–26.31 m in width** (median 11.55 m) and **1.59–24.69 m in height** (median 9.47 m). These figures describe only the digitized outlines, not a clearance guarantee or a representative distribution for the entire tube. The catalog also supplies plan maps and longitudinal profiles, but their drawing coordinates are not tied to these individual section stations or to the app's Earth DEM; Module 4 does not place sections on the map or reconstruct a 3-D tube from their order. `roofDepthBySectionM` and `stationCoordinates` are explicitly `null`.

Tomasi et al. report electrical-resistivity tomography interpretations of **14–29 m ceiling depth** for separate, elliptically shaped secondary channels near the surveyed transects. The app cites this as `literature-ert-inference` context. It is not a calculated depth of the 74 catalog sections, the reference-site center, or a discovered lunar void. A measured local roof depth would require co-registered interior ceiling and surface elevation; the open section files do not supply that pairing.

## One-meter terrain detail and obstacle-screen limits

`scripts/m4_extract_usgs_opr.py` reads the [USGS original-product-resolution DEM tile](https://www.sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/6197a1e0d34eb622f692c33d) for the Discovery candidate `cand-valles-marineris-05` and publishes `assets/data/m4/cand-valles-marineris-05-local-dem.json` (`M4LocalDemCrop` version 1). Download the [source GeoTIFF](https://prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com/StagedProducts/Elevation/OPR/Projects/NM_SouthEast_2018_D19/NM_SouthEast_B1_2018/TIFF/USGS_OPR_NM_SouthEast_2018_D19_13S_CS_59999_04500.tif), then run `python3 scripts/m4_extract_usgs_opr.py --input <downloaded-TIF>`. The script validates the reviewed 1 m tiled LZW float GeoTIFF, NAD83(2011) UTM 13N / NAVD88 projection, source SHA-256, and candidate-center crop. It uses only the Python standard library; the published values are rounded to centimeters for storage, which is **not** a vertical-accuracy claim. The [USGS 3DEP products](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program/) are public domain. The original USGS tile avoids relying on the separate Planetary Computer derived collection's ambiguous `proprietary` STAC license field.

`m4-local-terrain.mjs` compares each 1 m cell with four averages of opposite neighbors and reports the largest absolute deviation as a *terrain-break residual*. A planar slope yields zero by construction. The viewer can switch among elevation, 1 m Horn slope, 3×3 m local relief, and that residual; the red residual screen uses a user-set threshold (0.5 m by default). In the published patch, **2,465 of 159,201 measured interior cells** exceed 0.5 m. These are screening pixels, **not 2,465 known rocks or obstacles**. Cliffs, noise, ground-filtering, and interpolation can trigger them; objects above the bare-earth surface can be absent. The 400 m patch is shown as a dashed square in the 2,400 m regional map and does not alter route cost. The source's site-specific vertical accuracy and independent obstacle labels are not attached, and no rover geometry or wheel-clearance threshold is defined.

## Inputs, integration, and remaining acceptance work

- Input from Module 1: `assets/data/discovery/<targetId>.json` version 2, including ID, center, grid size/spacing, elevation, source dataset, native resolution, and provenance. The Discovery page passes target/candidate IDs in the route URL. The browser reads published files; it does not run a new satellite search.
- Fixed reference input: `assets/data/scenarios/earth/<earthId>.json`, target grid, Module 3 process history, and its M4 SAR pair. Discovery candidates load only their own M4 SAR pair. Reference evidence is never transferred to a discovery candidate.
- `route-math.mjs` owns pure route/slope calculations; `m4-hazards.mjs`, `m4-sar.mjs`, `m4-subsurface.mjs`, `m4-cave-geometry.mjs`, and `m4-local-terrain.mjs` own evidence interpretation; `route-explorer.js` owns DOM/canvas presentation.
- Route results remain local to Module 4. They are not yet in Mission Control, the shared dossier, or a mission export.

## Route evidence export

After a valid route calculation, users can download `M4RouteEvidenceGeoJSON` version 1. `m4-route-evidence.mjs` converts grid nodes to approximate WGS84 longitude/latitude inside the small Earth window and retains the exact row/column path. Each LineString carries the chosen slope/relief settings, distance, ascent/descent, route slope/relief/TRI summaries, DEM source and native/display resolution, candidate or reference ID, target ID, and caveats. If a matching spatial SAR pair exists, its scene IDs and links are included as **context only**; SAR does not alter route cost. The export records the input Discovery data version when available and its generation time. Coordinates are rounded to five decimals and are not surveyed waypoints.

## Remaining acceptance work

To meet the full module description in `revision-propuesta-y-referentes-2025.md`, the team still needs full-window vehicle-scale coverage, object-level/field labels for obstacle detection, a rover configuration with mobility limits, uncertainty and false-positive analysis for SAR/terrain screens, co-registered surface/interior surveys for local roof-depth calculations, and route evidence in the cross-module dossier. The open La Corona sections and one local 1 m Earth DEM close specific evidence gaps but do not validate mobility or reconstruct the tube. RADARSAT is not currently used: it should be added only if a specific product supplies coverage or capability absent from Sentinel-1. Unknown evidence stays unknown; a measured surface pit is not an inferred navigable tube.
