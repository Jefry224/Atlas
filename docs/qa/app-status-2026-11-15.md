# S.E.L.E.N.E. Ops — application status

**As of:** 2026-11-15

**Review basis:** local repository on `feature/module-4-route-explorer`, the published data files, code, documentation, and local checks. The Module 4 work is committed on this branch; this report does not describe a merged or deployed release.

## Executive status

S.E.L.E.N.E. Ops is a working exploratory prototype for finding and inspecting Earth terrain analogues of four lunar and Martian targets. It combines a precomputed Discovery search, fixed Earth–planetary comparison scenarios, physical analogy auditing, environmental process analysis, simulated Earth expeditions and target descents, a Flight Director, and a separate terrain route explorer. It can run with a local FastAPI backend or as a static site with archived fallback data.

**Challenge fit:** the prototype demonstrates the challenge's central idea—using open Earth and planetary data to identify candidate terrestrial analogues and present them in an interactive visualization. Its candidates are proposals for further investigation, not verified new field sites. The broader five-module acceptance plan is **not complete**: results do not yet follow one discovered candidate through every module and the final dossier. Module 4 now has candidate-specific spatial SAR context, 74 measured La Corona interior cross-sections, and one local 1 m Earth DEM crop. It still lacks a georeferenced 3-D tube, per-section roof depths, and a validated rover-scale obstacle detector. No percentage of completion is assigned because neither the [official challenge](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/permanent-moon-base-locations-and-mars/) nor the [team plan](../product/revision-propuesta-y-referentes-2025.md) defines a numerical scoring rubric.

## What a user can do now

1. Open **Mission Control** (`index.html`) to browse 14 documented Earth sites and four fixed Earth–Moon/Mars scenario pairs. Terrain mode supports a confirmed, multi-stop Earth rover expedition with inspections and a standalone JSON/Markdown field report. Destination mode supports mission preparation, a modeled final descent, operator authorization, and a simulated Earth/target rover comparison. The planetary target data include lunar and Martian elevation and thermal context; motion and vehicle behavior are simulations.
2. Open **Discovery** (`discovery.html`) to compare the four planetary targets with published scan results. The two-stage pipeline prefilters regions and refines candidate cells with JAXA AW3D30 terrain and climate context. The repository currently publishes **19 ranked candidates**: Marius Hills 4, Shackleton 5, Jezero 5, and Valles Marineris 5. Their files report 6,220 refined cells scanned in total. The page shows ranking components, coverage/caveats, provenance, and a GeoJSON download. It loads precomputed JSON; selecting a new location or running an arbitrary global scan in the browser is not implemented.
3. For a fixed scenario, inspect the **analogy audit and engineering calibration**: geometry, gravity, pressure, thermal differences, equipment assumptions, landing preparation/results, and source types. The UI can export a Module 2 dossier as JSON or Markdown, a mission JSON record, and a session workbook as XLSX. These destination reports currently use the fixed scenario path, not a newly discovered Discovery candidate. A separate rock/pit planning assessment uses explicit assumptions or a documented reference; it does not measure a new site's subsurface.
4. For fixed scenarios, inspect **current model-based Earth weather**, a 72-hour forecast, process windows for thermal cycles, wind/saltation, freezing or cold conditions, and a 12-month Sentinel-1 RTC backscatter history. With no Meteomatics credentials, the backend uses Open-Meteo; with no AI provider key, the Flight Director uses local rules. The process history is a scenario-level time series of window aggregates.
5. From a Discovery candidate, open **Route & Terrain Explorer** (`route-explorer.html`). Choose two cells in its Earth DEM window; compare distance-weighted and slope-weighted corridors under user-set slope and optional local-relief limits; inspect regional slope, TRI, relief, and modeled sun-angle terrain shadow. Download the route as GeoJSON with approximate coordinates, settings, resolution, sources, and caveats. All 19 published candidates and four fixed Earth references have separate georeferenced Sentinel-1 RTC backscatter and repeat-pass difference maps available for download as JSON. The Marius reference adds a 4 m LROC surface DTM around the documented pit, with elevation, slope, and relief views; the published 40 m pit depth is cited separately. The La Corona reference has 74 selectable measured cave cross-sections and separate literature ERT depth context. One Valles Discovery candidate has a 400 m USGS 1 m bare-earth DEM crop with slope, relief, and terrain-break screening views. Reference-site radar and subsurface evidence is never inherited by a discovery candidate.

## How this meets the challenge

The [challenge statement](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/permanent-moon-base-locations-and-mars/) asks for open Earth, Moon and Mars data to identify and characterize **new terrestrial analogues** for future bases or landing sites, and to present the findings as a map, visualization, or other tool.

| Challenge objective | Present evidence | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use open Earth and planetary data | JAXA AW3D30 and Copernicus DEM/Sentinel products on Earth; published lunar/Martian terrain and thermal products for targets; per-product provenance in the scenario and discovery data. | **Demonstrated** for the published datasets. Coverage and measurement scales differ across products. |
| Identify candidate Earth locations beyond the familiar references | The Discovery pipeline publishes 19 ranked Earth windows from configured scans, alongside four fixed comparison pairs. | **Demonstrated as candidate generation.** No literature/field review establishes that each location is genuinely novel or suitable for testing. The scan is not exhaustive over all Earth. |
| Characterize relevance to lunar/Martian operations | Terrain metrics, climate context, physical audit, process windows, candidate-specific surface radar context, regional route scenarios, cave-reference sections, and one local 1 m terrain patch. | **Partial.** Materials, detailed geology, validated mobility hazards, georeferenced subsurface structure, and roof-depth measurements remain missing. |
| Present findings as a usable visualization | Discovery ranking/map, synchronized Mission Control terrain, and the Route Explorer. | **Demonstrated locally.** Cross-module candidate continuity and the final consolidated dossier are incomplete. |

The team plan in [revision-propuesta-y-referentes-2025.md](../product/revision-propuesta-y-referentes-2025.md) is more ambitious than the challenge's core presentation requirement. Its five-module closeout asks for new-query execution, detector validation, spatial SAR and subsurface evidence, coherent candidate state across the console, and complete exports. Those acceptance conditions remain **open**.

## Module 4's concrete contribution

Module 4 turns a Discovery DEM window into an exploratory **terrain decision**: where could a regional corridor pass under chosen slope and relief assumptions, and what evidence is missing before anyone treats that corridor as navigable? A route result reports path, distance, ascent/descent, slope, relief, TRI, and caveats, and exports a georeferenced GeoJSON for review. Users can inspect DEM-derived hazard indicators, a separate terrain-shadow scenario, and the selected candidate's own spatial SAR pair. Fixed references additionally show aggregate temporal SAR history or cited cave/pit context. La Corona adds measured interior section outlines without implying a mapped underground path; one Valles candidate adds finer Earth surface morphology and a user-set terrain-break screen. The two paths keep their evidence attached to the correct window.

This is useful for **triage and follow-up planning**. A 37.5 m grid, often sampled from a 30 m DEM, does not resolve rocks, cracks, soil bearing strength, cave entrances, or rover-scale clearance. The 1 m USGS bare-earth patch resolves local topographic breaks in one 400 m window; it does not detect individual objects or validate vehicle clearance. The 4 m LROC crop resolves more lunar surface form near the Marius pit but has nodata inside it and cannot establish underground depth or navigability. La Corona's sections measure local interior shapes, but their stations and roof depths are unknown; the 14–29 m ERT inference concerns separate secondary channels. The Earth shadow layer considers terrain inside the window only. Paired Sentinel-1 rasters have 10 m source pixel spacing but about 20×22 m nominal radar resolution in the reviewed IW scenes; they show surface backscatter differences, not underground voids or validated hazards. See [Module 4 sources and limits](../modules/m4-routes.md).

## Highest-value remaining work

1. **Unify candidate identity across modules.** Pass a selected Discovery candidate into the audit, process view, Mission Control, and exports with the same ID, input version, provenance, and missing-data states.
2. **Validate the Module 4 evidence case.** The 23 published SAR pairs provide spatial context with orbit/polarization, masks, dates and resolution, but no independent labels prove that a 3 dB difference marks a hazard. The La Corona sections need station positions and a co-registered surface survey before calculating local roof depth. Extend 1 m or finer coverage beyond one candidate, acquire object/field labels and rover limits, and test false positives before making mobility claims.
3. **Validate the analogue claims.** Review candidate novelty and published field context; compare selected candidates with independent references and report false positives, uncertainty, and scale limits. Validate any obstacle/hazard detector before adding an operational label.
4. **Close the demonstration and dossier.** Show one end-to-end discovered-candidate journey and cases with missing data; include route and process evidence in consistent JSON/Markdown/PDF outputs; verify the deployed URL, source links, licensing, and presentation materials.
